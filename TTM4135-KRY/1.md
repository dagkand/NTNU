**QUESTION 1**

- confidentiality - C
Roughly equivalent to privacy, confidentiality measures are designed to prevent sensitive information from unauthorized access attempts.

- integrity - I
The consistency, accuracy and trustworthiness of data must be maintained over its entire lifecycle. Data must not be changed in transit, and steps must be taken to ensure it can't be altered by unauthorized people for example, in data breaches.

- availability - A
Information should be consistently and readily accessible for authorized parties. This involves properly maintaining hardware and technical infrastructure and systems that hold and display the information.

- entity authentication
Entity authentication is the process by which one entity (the verifier) is assured of the identity of a second entity (the claimant) that is participating in a protocol (see identity verification protocol).

- data origin authentication
Also known as message authentication, data origin authentication is an assurance that the source of the information is indeed verified. Data origin authentication guarantees data integrity because if a source is corroborated, then the data must not have been altered.

- non-repudiation
Non-repudiation means a user cannot deny (repudiate) having performed a transaction. It combines authentication and integrity: non-repudiation authenticates the identity of a user who performs a transaction, and ensures the integrity of that transaction.

- group generator
An element in a group that has the same order as the group. It is called generator, because all elements in the group can be written as a power of that elements. In cryptography, a group generator is a system that generates large prime numbers. It is used in public-key cryptography systems, such as the Diffie-Hellman key exchange, where the generator is used to create a public key. 

- finite field.
A finite field, also known as a Galois field, is a mathematical field that contains a finite number of elements. In cryptography, finite fields are used in various algorithms, including elliptic curve cryptography and advanced encryption standards (AES). They provide a mathematical framework for cryptographic operations, particularly in the context of public-key cryptography.

# Euclid's Algorithm for GCD

Let's find the GCD of 23 and 29.

## Step 1:
Start with \( a = 29 \) and \( b = 23 \).

## Step 2:
\[
\frac{29}{23} = 1 \text{ remainder } 6
\]
Set \( a = b \) and \( b = r \), so \( a = 23 \) and \( b = 6 \).

## Step 3:
\[
\frac{23}{6} = 3 \text{ remainder } 5
\]
Set \( a = b \) and \( b = r \), so \( a = 6 \) and \( b = 5 \).

## Step 4:
\[
\frac{6}{5} = 1 \text{ remainder } 1
\]
Set \( a = b \) and \( b = r \), so \( a = 5 \) and \( b = 1 \).

## Step 5:
\[
\frac{5}{1} = 5 \text{ remainder } 0
\]
Since the remainder \( r \) is zero, the GCD of 23 and 29 is 1.
